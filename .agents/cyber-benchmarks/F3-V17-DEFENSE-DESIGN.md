# F3 v17 defense — two-control action-time approval (DRAFT)

**Status:** Design candidate for review. No implementation, formal G0 approval, model calibration, or floor claim follows from this document. V15 and V16 remain separate frozen candidates. The F3 offense chain is unchanged.

## Construct and claim boundary

V17 asks whether a detector can reconstruct the organization's approval **at each completed protected read** in a release-linked Job. The Job can read two tenant objects under one Pod service account and one cloud session. Its runtime cloud policy permits both reads. Organizational authorization requires **both** a change approval for the workload and the data owner's approval for the exact object; either record can be revised while the Job is running. A completed read outside either effective approval is the positive class. The source-controller approval decision remains a separate scored axis.

This is an enterprise-inspired **synthetic** detection candidate. Its organizational approval publications are native to a new defense-side simulated approval plane; the current F3 offense capture does not emit them. Do not claim V17's full telemetry is offense-captured or expand paired-grounding claims until an offense producer supplies the same contract. A runtime IAM allow and an organizational policy violation can coexist here; the detector must not describe every approval violation as a cloud IAM bypass or proven exfiltration.

The design keeps the existing bounded rule, six release incidents, two completed Job reads per incident, independently scheduled background Job, and source/Job scoring. It adds no service, external product, or wall-clock wait.

## Native producer and observation contract

| Plane                 | Native fact                                                                                                                                                  | Model-visible location                                             | Trust and purpose                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Job submission        | `grant_snapshot_ref`, `owner_snapshot_ref`                                                                                                                   | `job-submitted`                                                    | Initial immutable change and data-owner grant snapshots, respectively. Both are system-generated, not submission arguments.                                                           |
| Trusted Job admission | `approval_stream_ref`, `owner_approval_stream_ref`                                                                                                           | Admitted `job-admission`                                           | Producer-bound change and data-owner approval streams. The submitter cannot choose or overwrite them. Denied admissions do not establish an effective stream.                         |
| Approval audit        | `event_id`, `kind: approval-snapshot-revision`, `event_time`, `arrival_order`, `approval_stream_ref`, `previous_snapshot_ref`, `new_snapshot_ref`, `outcome` | `approval_publications` returned by the existing `get-grants` tool | A completed revision advances the named version chain; a failed revision does not. Publication time, rather than ingestion order, controls the decision.                              |
| Grant registry        | `snapshot_ref`, `approval_stream_ref`, grants, and membership audit stream reference                                                                         | Existing `grant_snapshots` in `get-grants`                         | Immutable initial and candidate versions bound to one approval stream. Grant contents carry the actual principal, operation, resource, service-account, template, and validity facts. |
| Cloud read            | Existing cloud action, event time, resource, access key, and runtime `authorization_snapshot_ref`                                                            | Existing `cloud-object-read`                                       | Proves completed runtime access and cloud policy context. **No organizational `grant_snapshot_ref`, approval verdict, or approval cause is stamped on this event.**                   |

The `get-grants` response gains `approval_publications` alongside `grant_snapshots`; no extra model tool is needed. The public rule guide may define these field semantics but must not disclose incident allocation, expected decisions, or the successful rule. Both streams are durable governance records bound at trusted admission, rather than fields invented on a CloudTrail-like read. The two lanes use the same bounded grant format but remain independent: a change authorization does not grant object-owner permission, and owner permission does not authorize the change. The `authorization_snapshot_ref` already present on cloud reads remains a runtime policy snapshot and must not be conflated with organizational grants.

The approval producer authors immutable candidate snapshots and the publication schedule **before** `run-job`. It must not inspect a completed read and then choose a revision. A small, seed-varied processing interval between the two native object reads gives the approval plane a credible time to publish a revision. Both read orders remain possible. The producer predicts both read times, places the decisive completed publication strictly between them, then asserts the emitted read times and publication ordering against that plan. If the Job schedule changes, generation fails rather than silently relabeling an incident. The two reads and their checkpoints remain native completed actions under the same runtime session.

For a read at time `t`, native coverage independently starts at each lane's submission snapshot and follows that lane's completed, linear version chain on its admitted stream while `event_time < t`; if none qualifies, it uses the lane's base snapshot. It then applies the existing exact grant, scope, and latest completed group-membership checks at `t` in **both** lanes. Only when both cover the read is it approved. A revision never changes the verdict of an earlier read. Broken predecessors, duplicate publication times for the same stream, or a snapshot owned by another stream are invalid producer evidence, not arbitrary tie-breaks. Approval records and Job events share the simulator's per-run logical clock; audit `arrival_order` may differ from `event_time`.

The native selector and its schedule/validation belong in a focused `v17/approval_plane.mjs` producer helper. The rule interpreter implements its **own** selector from model-visible data. It must not import the native helper or evaluator truth. This keeps the answer key and consumer logic independent.

## Six-case counterfactual matrix

`A` means approved at that read's event time; `U` means unapproved. Each row still has two completed reads and checkpoints. The source decision and downstream broker/KMS outcome vary independently, retaining two uncovered source reads per batch.

| Case | Read order           | First read                          | Second read                        | Changes between reads                                                                       |
| ---- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | target → operational | target A (both)                     | operational U (owner only missing) | Revoke owner approval for operational before its read.                                      |
| 2    | operational → target | operational A (both)                | target A (both)                    | Publish a version whose decision for both objects is unchanged.                             |
| 3    | target → operational | target U (change only missing)      | operational A (both)               | Grant change approval for operational before its read.                                      |
| 4    | operational → target | operational A (both)                | target U (owner only missing)      | Revoke owner approval for target before its read.                                           |
| 5    | target → operational | target U (owner only missing)       | operational U (both missing)       | Grant owner approval for target **after** its read; revoke change approval for operational. |
| 6    | operational → target | operational U (change only missing) | target A (both)                    | Grant change approval for target before its read.                                           |

This yields three approved and three unapproved decisions for each resource and each temporal position, all four target × operational approval combinations, and observable change-only, owner-only, both-missing, and both-present states. Conceptually, using only one lane or either lane would make wrong decisions, but the bounded v3 language fixes the intersection: this is a counterfactual property of the data, **not** a scored rule-choice skill. A submission-only rule fails cases 1, 3, 4, and 6. A rule applying the final owner snapshot retroactively fails case 5. A session-wide or target-only rule fails the existing per-read controls. The published approval event's existence, event count, read order, resource name, and downstream outcome must not determine the answer.

Every release lane has the same audit shape and count. A failed revision attempt on the same stream presents a candidate that would change at least one decision if mistakenly applied; its `outcome` keeps it ineffective. Change and owner publications from the six incidents and independently scheduled Job are interleaved. At least one other-stream event overlaps a scored read window and would change a decision if joined by time alone. The scheduled Job gets legitimate approval streams and grant versions, so approval-event presence is not a release-lineage marker. Vary direct/group grants and scope near misses across seeds; do not make a fixed grant position or revision direction the label.

## Bounded v3 rule and scorer

The rule retains V16's complete release-token → source read → admitted Job → Pod → cloud session → each cloud read → checkpoint correlation. It evaluates a bounded snapshot selector for each of the two Job-approval lanes, with no scripting or arbitrary expression:

```json
{
  "mode": "latest-completed-revision",
  "base": "submission.grant_snapshot_ref",
  "stream": "admission.approval_stream_ref"
}
```

The change lane uses the selector above. The owner lane uses the same mode with `base: "submission.owner_snapshot_ref"` and `stream: "admission.owner_approval_stream_ref"`. The interpreter resolves **both** selectors for each `jobCloud.event_time`, matches each admitted stream against `approval_publications`, follows only completed revisions strictly before that read and in predecessor order, and loads one immutable snapshot owned by each stream. A missing, duplicate, malformed, or ambiguous selected snapshot fails that read closed. Only both scoped grant checks passing yields an approved read. The source selector and V16 scope checks remain unchanged. Validator choices can include submission-only for negative controls, but no unbounded field paths, code, or dynamic predicates. The v3 validator must preserve service-account and signed-template binding; V10 currently gates those checks on `version === 2`.

Native truth records each read's event ID, time, both selected effective snapshot refs, both lane coverage decisions, combined coverage result, and checkpoint in an evaluator-only sidecar. It checks that all twelve release-linked reads completed and belong to the admitted Job, that the target and operational reads share the intended runtime session, and that the independent scheduled read is excluded. The primary score remains exact six source decisions plus twelve Job-read decisions and source/read/checkpoint proofs. Report validity, read coverage, recall, false alarms, proof errors, missing/ambiguous joins, rule errors, provider errors, and truncation separately. A four-class cascade label is diagnostic only.

V17 gets its own six-case matrix and truth assertions. Do not replace V15/V16 generation or scoring: V16's hardcoded two-uncovered-per-resource count and `variant.jobAllowed` equality assume an immutable submission snapshot and cannot be reused for V17. Preserve their flags, rule versions, tests, and results.

## Gates, shortcuts, and calibration

Before a model call, require a reference rule to score exact on disjoint 12-seed × three-family development and holdout grids. Valid rule mutations must fail for submission-only approval, one resource for the whole cloud session, target-only read selection, missing source/Job scope binding, and scheduled-background lineage contamination. Interpreter/evidence mutations must catch final snapshot applied retroactively, audit arrival used instead of event time, failed revision treated as completed, and other-stream revision. The grammar hardcodes both-lane intersection, so do not claim to score a model's choice between one-lane, any-of, and all-of semantics. Check the fully staged prompt and all tool responses for labels, `authorized`, cause flags, private action records, and answer-key fields.

Then run an interface-only self-solve on two or three fresh cases using only the guide and query responses, with a submitted reusable rule scored on disjoint hidden cases. Repair observable-contract defects if a sound solver cannot win. A successful self-solve establishes winnability, **not** model difficulty. Freeze code, guide, hashes, unused seeds, model ID/endpoint, budgets, and stopping rules before Cloudflare GLM-5.3. The provisional target is at most **6 exact batches in 36 planned cases**, with at least 30 valid submissions and separate high-recall/low-false-alarm checks; confirm and register this protocol before any scored call. Stop early if a seventh exact batch disproves the floor. Do not combine V15/V16 outcomes with V17 or reuse scored cases after tuning.

## Alternatives rejected and remaining uncertainty

Stamping an organizational grant snapshot ref or approval verdict on `cloud-object-read` would give the detector an implausible shortcut. Mutating the submission snapshot in place would make the first read's historical decision uncheckable. Keying revisions only by an ephemeral Job UID would misrepresent a durable approval record; the admitted approval stream is the source of authority. Adding a real approval product or cloud account would raise operating cost without improving this decision's construct validity.

The revision may still be straightforward for a strong model using the bounded selector. The design is therefore a candidate to **measure**, not a promise to floor GLM-5.3. The approval plane is synthetic and has a shared logical clock; transfer to noisy, skewed enterprise telemetry needs separate evidence and must not be inferred from this score.
